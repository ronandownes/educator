## Emotional Wellbeing and Relationships

### Name what may be affecting the learner, then respond within the teacher's role.

**emotion → regulation → connection → participation → learning**

### 💛 Emotional Wellbeing


| Term | Meaning in practice |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Emotional literacy** | Recognising, naming, understanding and communicating emotions so that an appropriate response becomes possible. |
| **Anxiety or distress** | Signs that a student may be worried, overwhelmed or less ready to learn; I notice and respond without diagnosing. |
| **Confidence and self-worth** | The student's belief that they are capable, valued and entitled to participate. |
| **Resilience and recovery** | Regaining engagement after difficulty, disappointment or conflict, often with appropriate support. |
| **Change in presentation** | A noticeable change in mood, behaviour, attendance, participation, relationships or quality of work. |


### 🧭 Regulation


| Term | Meaning in practice |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Self-regulation** | Managing emotion, attention and behaviour sufficiently to participate and learn. |
| **Co-regulation** | A calm, predictable adult helping a student regain regulation before expecting independent control. |
| **Triggers and escalation** | Events or conditions that increase stress, frustration or dysregulation. |
| **Calm and de-escalation** | Reducing pressure, audience and verbal demand while allowing time and a dignified route back. |
| **Predictability** | Clear routines, expectations and transitions that reduce uncertainty and support regulation. |


### 🤝 Connection


| Term | Meaning in practice |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Relational safety** | The student experiences the classroom and teacher as respectful, consistent and free from humiliation. |
| **Sense of belonging** | Feeling accepted, included and recognised as a valued member of the class. |
| **Trusted adult** | A known adult whom the student feels able to approach when support is needed. |
| **Peer relationships** | The friendships, tensions and group dynamics that may support or obstruct participation. |
| **School connectedness** | The student's wider sense of attachment to school, its people and its routines. |


### 🌱 Participation


| Term | Meaning in practice |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Readiness to learn** | Being emotionally, cognitively and practically available to engage with the lesson. |
| **Help-seeking** | Recognising a difficulty and asking for support in an appropriate way. |
| **Re-engagement** | Returning to learning after absence, conflict, distress or withdrawal. |
| **Learner agency** | Having meaningful voice, choice and responsibility within clear teacher direction. |
| **Participation** | Being able to contribute, attempt, communicate and remain involved in learning. |


### Interview Line

**I try to recognise what may be affecting the student's readiness to learn, support regulation and belonging, and use the school's pastoral structures where concerns extend beyond my role.**

## Mathematical Expectations

### Make the mathematical standard visible, not assumed.

I make expectations explicit around **showing working, reasoning, notation, mathematical language and clear mathematical communication**.

**working → reasoning → notation → language → communicate**

## Prior Knowledge as a Barrier

### Prior knowledge becomes a barrier when it is incomplete, fragile or incorrect.

Students may bring **gaps, misconceptions, overgeneralised rules or procedures learned by rote without understanding**.

The barrier is not simply that they have prior learning. It is that the new learning may be resting on a weak or inaccurate foundation.

I surface that knowledge quickly, check it and, where necessary, rebuild it before attaching new learning.

**surface → check → correct → connect**

## If They Ask: What Do You Mean by Prior Learning?

### I mean the knowledge the new learning depends on — and whether it is secure enough to support the next step.

A student may remember a rule but not understand why it works, may have a misconception, or may have only part of the prerequisite knowledge.

So I check what is actually secure rather than assuming that previously taught means securely learned.

## Barriers to Learning

### A barrier is anything that unnecessarily gets between the learner and the intended learning.

**prior knowledge and misconceptions → language and literacy → processing and working memory → attention and organisation → communication → sensory and regulation → confidence and anxiety → physical and environmental**

A useful distinction is:

- **no prior knowledge** → gap
- **patchy prior knowledge** → weak foundation
- **incorrect prior knowledge** → misconception
- **rote prior learning** → procedure without meaning
- **secure prior knowledge** → resource, not barrier
