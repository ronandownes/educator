> **Synthetic-data notice:** This class is entirely imaginary. The numbers and needs below are invented for planning and interview practice and do not describe real pupils.

## Class context

30 students with strong overall attainment and pressure to maintain Higher Level through the examination year.

## Strengths and interests

High fluency, positive mathematical identity and several students capable of elegant generalisation.

## Fictional learning and access profile

2 students with dyslexia; 3 with ADHD; 5 experiencing performance anxiety; 4 students are currently at risk of changing level because prerequisite gaps have accumulated.

## Starting evidence

Routine topic tests are strong, but unfamiliar geometry and multi-stage probability expose weak planning and justification.

## Planned teaching response

Use non-routine problems routinely, model how to plan before calculating, run targeted prerequisite clinics and treat level decisions as evidence-based rather than a verdict on ability.

## Assessment and review loop

Untimed reasoning first, then timed transfer; cumulative papers; individual gap maps and review meetings.
