> **Synthetic-data notice:** This class is entirely imaginary. The numbers and needs below are invented for planning and interview practice and do not describe real pupils.

## Class context

28 students following the common introductory course; wide variation in primary-school experience and confidence.

## Strengths and interests

Strong curiosity, good oral discussion and several students who enjoy puzzles, sport statistics and coding.

## Fictional learning and access profile

4 students with identified dyslexia; 3 with ADHD; 1 autistic student; 2 EAL learners; 5 showing insecure multiplicative reasoning. Six students demonstrate rapid recall and unusually strong generalisation.

## Starting evidence

An opening rich task shows that calculation accuracy is stronger than explanation. Fraction and place-value representations vary sharply; the high-attaining group finishes routine items quickly but has had little experience of proof.

## Planned teaching response

Use common core tasks with multiple representations, pre-teach essential vocabulary, provide chunked instructions and visual models, and extend through proof, counterexample and generalisation rather than extra routine questions.

## Assessment and review loop

Weekly retrieval; brief student explanations; misconception log; October and Christmas review of both support and stretch.
