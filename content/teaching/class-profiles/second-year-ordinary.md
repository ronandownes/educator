> **Synthetic-data notice:** This class is entirely imaginary. The numbers and needs below are invented for planning and interview practice and do not describe real pupils.

## Class context

24 students in a newly formed Ordinary Level group. Several moved from a common first-year arrangement and confidence is uneven.

## Strengths and interests

Good engagement with financial, measurement and data contexts; supportive classroom relationships.

## Fictional learning and access profile

3 students with dyslexia; 2 with ADHD; 1 EAL learner; 6 with persistent fraction and ratio gaps. Four students may be capable of returning to Higher Level if algebraic foundations strengthen.

## Starting evidence

Diagnostic work shows overreliance on memorised steps and weak links among tables, graphs and equations.

## Planned teaching response

Build a shared conceptual model before procedure; use graduated practice and regular mixed retrieval; keep challenge visible and discuss level movement at agreed review points.

## Assessment and review loop

Short exit checks, cumulative quizzes every two weeks, one common task per half-term and a level-review evidence folder.
