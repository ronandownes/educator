But I **wouldn't make SEN/AEN four giant columns**. Your instinct there is right. Have one compact **AEN / Access** column, and let the *actual teaching consequence* appear elsewhere.

So you see:

**Dyslexia → reading/access → vocabulary/visual model/chunking**

rather than:

**Dyslexia ✓**

and nothing else.

That is much closer to professional practice.

## Colour Coding

Colour could make this excellent, provided it means something consistently:

🟦 **Access / AEN**  
🟨 **Prior-learning gap / misconception**  
🟩 **Strength / secure**  
🟪 **Stretch / high attainment**  
🟧 **Language / EAL / literacy**

I would avoid making **red = SEN**. Red should probably mean something requiring immediate instructional attention, not a learner characteristic.

And then your eye can scan the class and immediately notice:

**A, F, L, T → reading/access**  
**B, H, R → attention/organisation**  
**D, Q → language access**  
**C, G, J, P, V, Y → stretch**  
**B, I, M, S, W → multiplicative reasoning**

That begins to look like an actual teacher's working class profile.

## Most important: make the vocabulary clickable

This is where your education site becomes more than a static interview notebook.

If the row says:

**Working memory**  
**Retrieval**  
**Scaffolding**  
**Pre-teach**  
**Misconception**  
**Generalisation**  
**Counterexample**  
**Multiple representations**  
**Chunking**  
**Elicitation**  
**Diagnostic assessment**

those terms can get your little blue glossary underline.

Click **“generalisation”** and you get:

**Definition → Mathematics example → 6 interview sentences → related terms**

So the class map itself becomes a **memory-retrieval device**.

And underneath the whole table I'd retain a tiny summary:

> **What this changes in my teaching:** common high-expectation curriculum; targeted access rather than separate work; formative assessment identifies the next barrier; extension comes through reasoning, proof, counterexample and generalisation.

That gives you **student → barrier/strength → response → assessment**, which is exactly the chain you want to be able to articulate at interview.

I think the A–Z matrix should become the main object, with your existing prose collapsed underneath as **“Class Profile Summary”** rather than the other way around. It will look far more like a living classroom and much less like an AI-generated paragraph.
