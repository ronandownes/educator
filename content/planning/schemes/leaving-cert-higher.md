## Why a separate scheme

Higher Level needs substantially different pacing and demand. Faster coverage is useful only when it buys time for deeper understanding, connected reasoning and sustained work on unfamiliar problems. The route remains responsive: acceleration follows evidence, not assumption.

## Fifth Year

| Period | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Aug–Oct | Algebra, functions, equations and proof habits |
| Nov–Dec | Coordinate geometry and transformations between representations |
| Jan–Feb | Trigonometry and geometry with deduction and modelling |
| Mar–Apr | Statistics and probability with critical interpretation |
| May | Connected problems, cumulative assessment and repair |

## Sixth Year

| Period | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Aug–Oct | Calculus, functions and algebraic synthesis |
| Nov–Dec | Sequences, financial mathematics and modelling |
| Jan–Feb | Complete remaining content; extended unfamiliar problems |
| Mar–Apr | Interleaved papers, proof, strategy and precision |
| May | Targeted refinement rather than indiscriminate repetition |

## Protecting both access and ambition

- Compact review when mastery is secure; do not reteach everything to everyone.
- Offer support clinics and prerequisite retrieval without lowering the main demand.
- Watch for students whose procedural speed conceals weak concepts.
- Keep transfer decisions evidence-based and reversible where possible.
- Use Desmos or GeoGebra when they reveal structure, support conjecture or test a model—not as decoration.
