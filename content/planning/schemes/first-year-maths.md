## The central decision

First year should be common without becoming slow. The same mathematical idea can have a low floor and a high ceiling: all students meet the core representation and reasoning, while depth comes through justification, generalisation, unfamiliar problems and connections.

## Indicative year map

| Period | Mathematical focus | Unifying emphasis | Evidence of learning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aug–Sept | Transition, number sense, place value, estimation and diagnostic tasks | Representation; communication | Short diagnostic conversations, written explanations and error analysis |
| Oct | Integers, operations, factors, multiples and proportional thinking | Building blocks; connections | Retrieval checks and rich tasks with multiple strategies |
| Nov–Dec | Fractions, decimals, percentages and ratio | Representation; problem solving | Comparison tasks, models and contextual problems |
| Jan–Feb | Algebraic thinking, patterns, equality and generalising | Generalisation and proof | Rules in words, tables, diagrams and symbols |
| Mar | Geometry, measurement, construction and spatial reasoning | Connections; communication | Accurate construction, explanation and practical application |
| Apr–May | Data, probability, integrated investigations and consolidation | Problem solving; representation | Small investigation, cumulative assessment and reflection |

## Preventing the common course from becoming a ceiling

- Use common core questions, then deepen rather than merely accelerate.
- Ask for a second method, a proof, a counterexample or a general rule.
- Use non-routine problems before assuming that rapid calculation equals deep understanding.
- Track high attainment as carefully as gaps and misconceptions.
- Keep movement between future levels open; do not label students prematurely.

## Review points

Review the scheme at October mid-term, Christmas and Easter. Record what was securely learned, what needs retrieval, which students need additional access or stretch, and whether the next sequence still makes sense.
