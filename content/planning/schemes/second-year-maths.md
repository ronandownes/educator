## Shared spine, genuinely different pathways

Both pathways revisit core ideas and keep movement possible. Higher Level should move faster where evidence supports it and spend the gained time on deeper reasoning, more demanding algebra, unfamiliar problems and proof. Ordinary Level should not be reduced to disconnected procedures: it needs secure concepts, purposeful practice and repeated success.

## Indicative sequence

| Period | Shared mathematical focus | Ordinary Level emphasis | Higher Level emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aug–Sept | Retrieval and diagnostic review | Repair essential number and proportional gaps | Compact retrieval followed by connected challenge |
| Sept–Oct | Algebra, equations and functions | Meaning of equality, substitution and graphical links | Multi-step manipulation, generalisation and multiple representations |
| Nov | Geometry and trigonometric foundations | Measurement, construction and clear method | Deduction, justification and unfamiliar configurations |
| Dec–Jan | Ratio, rates, percentages and finance | Fluency in realistic contexts | Reverse and compound problems; modelling assumptions |
| Feb–Mar | Statistics and probability | Read, represent and interpret data accurately | Compare representations, critique claims and reason probabilistically |
| Apr–May | Integrated problem solving and consolidation | Scaffolded cumulative problems | Extended non-routine problems and proof |

## Pacing safeguards

- Run common review points without forcing the classes to be on the same textbook page.
- Treat transfer between levels as a planned decision supported by evidence.
- Record prerequisite gaps separately from the current topic.
- For Higher Level, avoid spending weeks repeating mastered material.
- For Ordinary Level, revisit strategically without restarting the whole course.
