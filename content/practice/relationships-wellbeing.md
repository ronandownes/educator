## What role do relationships play in learning?

### Trust is not an alternative to expectations; it is what makes expectations workable.

Students are more willing to take intellectual risks, ask for help and recover from mistakes when they know the teacher is consistent and respectful.

I try to know students as learners, notice changes in engagement and use restorative conversations when relationships need repair.



*"I expect excellence because I trust you can reach it, and I'll help you get there."*

**trust → risk → participate → persist → learn**

🤝 → 🙋 → 💬 → 🌱 → 🎯

## How do you establish positive relationships with a new class?

### Warmth and structure should be visible from the first lesson.

I learn names, greet students, show interest in them as learners and establish clear routines. I explain expectations positively and follow through calmly and fairly.

Students should experience me as approachable and predictable: someone who notices effort, listens properly and maintains the boundaries that allow everyone to learn.

**names → routines → interest → consistency → trust**

👤 → 🔄 → 👀 → ⚖️ → 🤝

## What is the subject teacher's pastoral role?

### The subject teacher often sees the first small signs that something has changed.

I notice attendance, engagement, peer dynamics and sudden changes in work, mood or behaviour. I may check in privately with the student and pass relevant concerns through the school's tutor, year-head, pastoral, AEN or safeguarding structures.

I do not try to act as counsellor. I contribute careful observations, maintain appropriate boundaries and use the school's support system.

**notice → listen → record → communicate → support**

👀 → 🎧 → 📝 → 💬 → 🤲

## What does wellbeing look like in your classroom?

### Wellbeing is visible when students feel safe, respected, connected and able to participate in learning.

I contribute through predictable routines, respectful language, appropriate challenge, opportunities to succeed and a classroom where mistakes can be discussed without humiliation.

The Junior Cycle indicators—**Active, Responsible, Connected, Resilient, Respected and Aware**—give me a useful lens. Wellbeing is not an extra activity added to teaching; it is also reflected in how students experience the lesson.

**safe → respected → connected → participate → grow**

🛡️ → ❤️ → 🤝 → 🙋 → 🌱

## How do you support student voice and belonging?

### Student voice means meaningful influence, not the removal of teacher direction.

I listen to students' experiences of learning and offer appropriate choice in examples, methods, roles or ways of showing understanding. I use class discussion, check-ins and feedback to identify barriers that may not be visible to me.

I also watch whose voice is missing. Participation should not depend only on confidence or speed.

**listen → include → choose → participate → belong**

🎧 → 🫶 → ↔️ → 🙋 → ❤️

## What would you do if you were concerned about a student's wellbeing?

### Notice the change, respond calmly and use the support structure early.

I distinguish a pattern from a single difficult moment and record factual observations where appropriate. I speak to the student privately, listen without diagnosing and avoid promising something I may not be able to keep confidential.

I then pass the concern through the appropriate tutor, year-head, pastoral, AEN or safeguarding route and follow up within my role.

**notice → check in → record → refer → follow up**

👀 → 💬 → 📝 → ➡️ → 🔄

## What would you do if a student made a safeguarding disclosure?

### Listen, reassure, record and report—do not investigate.

I remain calm, listen carefully and take the student seriously. I do not promise secrecy, ask leading questions or conduct my own investigation.

I record the concern factually, using the student's own words where possible, and report it immediately in line with the school's child-protection procedures to the Designated Liaison Person or appropriate safeguarding route.

**listen → reassure → record → report**

🎧 → 🤲 → 📝 → 🛡️

## How would you support a student with poor attendance or disengagement?

### Attendance and disengagement are information before they are judgement.

I welcome the student back without a public interrogation and provide a manageable route into the current learning. I notice patterns, check whether there is a learning, relationship, wellbeing or access barrier and communicate through the school's attendance and pastoral structures.

The aim is to reconnect the student with learning while keeping expectations clear.

**welcome → reconnect → identify → communicate → monitor**

👋 → 🔗 → 🔎 → 💬 → 👀

## How would you repair a relationship after conflict?

### Restore dignity, clarify responsibility and create a route back into learning.

I allow the situation to settle and speak privately where possible. I listen, explain the impact of what happened, restate the boundary and agree what needs to happen next.

Restorative practice is not the absence of consequences. It combines accountability with repairing the relationship and restoring the student's participation.

**calm → listen → account → repair → return**

⚖️ → 🎧 → 🧭 → 🔧 → 🔄

## What would you do if a student reported bullying behaviour?

### Take it seriously, protect the student and follow the school's Bí Cineálta procedures.

I listen calmly, avoid making promises about the outcome and record the concern accurately. I report it promptly through the school's agreed anti-bullying structure rather than trying to resolve a potentially complex pattern informally by myself.

I continue to support the student's safety, participation and dignity and provide relevant observations as the matter is addressed.

**listen → record → report → support → monitor**

🎧 → 📝 → ➡️ → 🤲 → 👀

## How do you work with parents and colleagues to support wellbeing?

### Share relevant evidence, agree the next step and keep the student's needs at the centre.

I communicate factually and respectfully, beginning with specific observations rather than labels or assumptions. I also recognise strengths and what is already working.

Within appropriate confidentiality, I collaborate with parents, tutors, year heads, guidance, AEN staff, SNAs and management so that support is coordinated rather than fragmented. I record and review agreed actions where required.

**observe → communicate → collaborate → act → review**

👀 → 💬 → 🤝 → 🔧 → 🔄

## How do you balance care with high expectations?

### Care and high expectations strengthen each other.

Knowing a student's circumstances may change the route, the support or the time needed; it does not mean abandoning the learning or the boundary.

I combine empathy with clear expectations, make appropriate adjustments and help the student take the next achievable step. That protects dignity while still communicating belief in the student's capacity.

**understand → adapt → expect → support → progress**
