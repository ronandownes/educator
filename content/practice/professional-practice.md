## School Ethos and Mission
**Key line: I want to understand what the school says it values and how those values are lived in practice.**

For interview preparation, I look at the school's mission, patronage, characteristic spirit, student profile, programmes, policies, extracurricular life and current priorities. I then connect my own practice to that context rather than giving a generic answer about ethos.

Ethos should also appear naturally in answers about inclusion, relationships, classroom management, wellbeing and contribution to school life.

## School Types, Sector and Ethos
**Key line: Keep school sector and school ethos separate — they overlap, but they are not the same thing.**

A **voluntary secondary school** is a school-sector and governance category. **Denominational** or **multi-denominational** describes patronage or characteristic spirit. Many voluntary secondary schools have a denominational tradition, but the terms are not interchangeable.

For my notes, I want to distinguish and compare the main settings I have worked in or may interview for:

- Voluntary secondary schools
- ETB schools / community colleges
- Community schools
- Comprehensive schools
- Denominational settings
- Multi-denominational / inclusive settings

The useful interview question is not simply "What type of school is this?" but **"What does that structure and ethos mean for how I would contribute here?"**

## How do you work with colleagues?
**Key line: A student experiences a school, not a collection of isolated classrooms.**

I share information appropriately, collaborate with subject departments, AEN staff, SNAs, year heads and management, and use common approaches where consistency benefits the student. I also value feedback on my own practice.

## How do you contribute beyond your timetable?
**Key line: Contribution should use my strengths and meet a genuine school need.**

My experience includes examination work, digital-resource development, subject and SEN initiatives, practical use of educational technology and wider participation in school activities. I look for ways to contribute that are sustainable and useful rather than simply listing activities.

## Extracurricular Contribution
Typical interview questions here include:

- What extracurricular activities could you bring to the school?
- How have you contributed outside your classroom in previous schools?
- How could your Mathematics, Science, coding, data or technology interests add to school life?
- How would you support STEM, competitions, clubs or student projects?

## Interview Opener and Closer
**Opener:** a short account of who I am as a teacher, the experience I bring, what matters in my practice and why this particular post or school makes sense.

**Closer:** reconnect my experience to the needs and ethos of the school, identify what I can contribute, and finish with clear interest in the role rather than introducing an entirely new answer.

## Questions for the School
Prepare two or three genuine questions that show interest in the actual school rather than questions whose answers are already obvious from the website.

Useful areas include:

- the department or team I would be joining
- priorities for the post in the first year
- SEN / AEN structures and collaboration
- subject development, STEM or extracurricular opportunities
- professional development and whole-school priorities

Where I have researched a particular school, keep those school-specific notes separate and link them from here.
