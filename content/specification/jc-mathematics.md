## Overview

The specification develops mathematical proficiency through one **Unifying strand** and four contextual strands:

- Number
- Geometry and trigonometry
- Algebra and functions
- Statistics and probability

It is designed for a minimum of **240 hours** of timetabled student engagement across the three years of junior cycle.

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Aim — mathematical proficiency</summary>

The aim is to provide relevant and challenging opportunities for all students to become mathematically proficient. The specification describes five interconnected components:

- conceptual understanding
- procedural fluency
- strategic competence
- adaptive reasoning
- productive disposition

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Learning outcomes — how to read them</summary>

Learning outcomes describe the knowledge, understanding, skills and values students should be able to demonstrate by the end of junior cycle.

Most outcomes apply to all students, with additional Higher Level outcomes identified in the specification. The outcomes are three-year outcomes: they are not intended to be treated as isolated items that are permanently "completed" once encountered.

</details>

## Unifying Strand

The Unifying strand permeates all four contextual strands. It has no separate body of content; its learning outcomes underpin mathematical activity throughout the course.

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Building blocks — U.1 to U.3</summary>

- **U.1** Recall and demonstrate understanding of the fundamental concepts and procedures underpinning each strand.
- **U.2** Apply procedures accurately, effectively and appropriately.
- **U.3** Recognise equality as a relationship in which two mathematical expressions have the same value.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Representation — U.4</summary>

- **U.4** Represent a mathematical situation in a variety of ways — numerically, algebraically, graphically, physically and in words — and interpret, analyse and compare those representations.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Connections — U.5 to U.6</summary>

- **U.5** Make connections within and between strands.
- **U.6** Make connections between mathematics and the real world.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Problem solving — U.7 to U.10</summary>

- **U.7** Make sense of a problem and, where necessary, mathematise the situation.
- **U.8** Apply knowledge and skills to solve problems, including decomposing or simplifying them appropriately.
- **U.9** Interpret a solution in terms of the original question.
- **U.10** Evaluate possible solutions, their reasonableness, improvements and limitations.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Generalisation and proof — U.11 to U.12</summary>

- **U.11** Generate general mathematical statements or conjectures from specific instances.
- **U.12** Generate and evaluate mathematical arguments and proofs.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Communication — U.13</summary>

- **U.13** Communicate mathematics effectively: justify reasoning, interpret results, explain conclusions, and use mathematical language and notation precisely.

</details>

## Number

The Number strand develops representations of number, arithmetic operations, indices, factors and multiples, rational-number equivalence, proportionality, patterns and sets. It also lays groundwork for the transition from arithmetic to algebra.

<details class="study-level">
<summary>N.1 — Number representations and arithmetic operations</summary>

Students investigate operations in natural numbers, integers and rational numbers using models such as number lines, decomposition and equal groups; properties of operations; index notation and index laws; factors, multiples and primes; accuracy and rounding; and scientific notation.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>N.2 — Fractions, decimals, percentages, ratio and financial mathematics</summary>

Students convert flexibly between fractions, decimals and percentages; use ratio and proportion; and solve money-related problems including bills, VAT, profit and loss, percentage profit or loss, cost and selling price, compound interest, income tax, net pay, value-for-money calculations, mark-up and margin.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>N.3 — Proportionality</summary>

Students use absolute and relative comparison and solve proportionality problems, including currency conversion and average speed, distance and time.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>N.4 — Numerical patterns</summary>

Students analyse numerical patterns using representations including tables and graphs and continue such patterns.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>N.5 — Sets</summary>

Students work with the concept of a set, set equality, listing and rule-based descriptions, Venn diagrams, notation and terminology, and operations including intersection, union, difference and complement.

</details>

## Geometry & Trigonometry

This strand develops properties of two- and three-dimensional shapes, measurement, geometry, proof, trigonometry, coordinate geometry and transformations.

<details class="study-level">
<summary>GT.1 — Measure and time</summary>

Students calculate, interpret and apply units of measure and time.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>GT.2 — 2D shapes and 3D solids</summary>

Students draw and interpret scaled diagrams and nets, and calculate perimeter, area, volume, surface area and curved surface area for specified plane figures and solids, including appropriate use of π.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>GT.3 — Geometry, constructions and proof</summary>

Students engage with prescribed constructions, axioms, theorems, corollaries and converses from Geometry for Post-Primary School Mathematics; use and explain the language of theorem, proof, axiom, corollary, converse and implication; and create and evaluate geometrical proofs at the appropriate level.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>GT.4 — Trigonometric ratios</summary>

Students evaluate and use sine, cosine and tangent in right-angled triangles, together with their inverses, for angles between 0° and 90°.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>GT.5 — Coordinate geometry</summary>

Students investigate distance, midpoint, slope, intersections, parallel and perpendicular lines, line graphs, rate of change, intercepts and equations of lines in the prescribed forms.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>GT.6 — Transformations</summary>

Students recognise and draw images under translation, central symmetry, axial symmetry and rotation, and identify axes of symmetry.

</details>

## Algebra & Functions

This strand develops patterns and relationships, variables, expressions, equality, equations, factorisation, formulae and functions using words, symbols, tables, diagrams and graphs.

<details class="study-level">
<summary>AF.1 — Patterns and relationships</summary>

Students investigate linear, quadratic, doubling and tripling patterns in numerical, spatial and real-world contexts; represent them in tables and graphs; generalise them in words and algebra; and categorise them using defining characteristics.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>AF.2 — Variables, expressions and equality</summary>

Students generate and interpret expressions, evaluate expressions for given variable values, and use equality to generate and interpret equations.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>AF.3 — Equivalent expressions and factorisation</summary>

Students use properties of arithmetic operations and factorisation to add, subtract, simplify, multiply and divide algebraic expressions and move flexibly between expanded and factorised forms at the appropriate level.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>AF.4 — Equations and inequalities</summary>

Students select suitable strategies — graphical, numerical, algebraic, trial-and-improvement or working backwards — to solve linear and quadratic equations, simultaneous equations and linear inequalities at the appropriate level.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>AF.5 — Quadratic equations from roots</summary>

Students generate quadratic equations given integer roots.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>AF.6 — Changing the subject of a formula</summary>

Students apply the relationship between operations and the order of operations, including brackets and exponents, to change the subject of a formula.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>AF.7 — Functions</summary>

Students develop the concept of a function; represent and interpret functions graphically, diagrammatically, verbally and algebraically; use function language and notation; use graphs to find approximate solutions; and connect graph shape with the story of a phenomenon.

</details>

## Statistics & Probability

This strand develops sample spaces, probability, relative frequency, statistical enquiry, data displays, summary statistics and critical interpretation of data.

<details class="study-level">
<summary>SP.1 — Outcomes and sample spaces</summary>

Students generate sample spaces systematically, including tree diagrams for successive events and two-way tables for independent events, and use the fundamental principle of counting in authentic problems.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>SP.2 — Random events and probability</summary>

Students understand probability on a 0–1 scale, calculate probabilities for equally likely outcomes, and use relative frequency to estimate probability while recognising the effect of increasing the number of trials.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>SP.3 — Statistical investigation</summary>

Students generate a statistical question; plan and collect unbiased representative data; classify data; choose, draw and interpret appropriate displays; calculate and interpret summary statistics; evaluate displays; identify misuses of statistics; and discuss assumptions and limitations of conclusions.

</details>

## Assessment & Reporting

Assessment is presented as part of the learning process. It involves gathering, interpreting and using information about learning for purposes including feedback, differentiation, identifying strengths and difficulties, recording achievement and reporting progress.

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Assessment principles</summary>

- use assessment methods that are fit for purpose, timely and relevant
- provide opportunities for student reflection and active participation
- use success criteria, peer assessment, self-assessment and teacher assessment
- provide focused feedback that identifies next steps
- use summative assessment as one element of a broader assessment approach

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>CBA 1 — Mathematical investigation</summary>

Students complete a mathematical investigation over a three-week period using a problem-solving cycle. It is completed at the end of second year and followed by a Subject Learning and Assessment Review meeting.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>CBA 2 — Statistical investigation</summary>

Students complete a statistical investigation over a three-week period using the statistical enquiry cycle. It is completed at the end of the first term of third year and followed by a Subject Learning and Assessment Review meeting.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Inclusive assessment</summary>

Reasonable accommodations may be used where a student has a specific physical or learning difficulty, provided the arrangements are consistent with supports used in the student's learning throughout the year.

</details>

## Appendix B — Geometry for Post-Primary School Mathematics

<details class="study-level">
<summary>At a glance</summary>

Appendix B provides the agreed post-primary geometry framework: terminology, definitions, five explicit school-level axioms, theorems, corollaries, converses, propositions, constructions and guidance on the progression from investigation and discovery towards formal proof.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Five axioms</summary>

1. Two Points Axiom
2. Ruler Axiom
3. Protractor Axiom
4. Congruent Triangles — SAS + ASA + SSS
5. Axiom of Parallels

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Teaching approach — discovery to proof</summary>

The geometry guidance recommends practical work and investigation before formal theory. Students first encounter geometrical results through exploration and discovery, consider whether examples are sufficient evidence, develop conjectures and informal justifications, and then progress towards formal mathematical proof.

</details>

<details class="study-level">
<summary>Prescribed constructions — Junior Cycle</summary>

At Junior Cycle Ordinary Level, students study constructions 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15. Higher Level adds constructions 3 and 7.

</details>
